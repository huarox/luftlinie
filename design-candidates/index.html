Nine directions for Luftlinie

Each shows the same three screens: the landing, the live distance (the screen players stare at), and the lobby code. All follow “maximalist minimalism”: almost nothing on screen, but what’s there is huge, confident and finished. Pick one, or combine parts (for example C’s dial with A’s type).

A. Keynote

Apple product page. White and black, one gradient, giant type.

Light landing, dark game. The only colour is a blue-to-pink gradient used on exactly one thing per screen: a headline word on the landing, the time bar in game. The distance is a 150 px chrome numeral fading into the dark.

B. Glass

visionOS materials. Frosted panels floating over a living colour field.

Everything sits on blurred glass above a soft aurora that slowly shifts hue as you get closer (cool far away, warm near). Rich, tactile, still very empty. Heaviest on GPU, so it needs a reduced-transparency fallback.

C. Wayfinder

Apple Watch Ultra. Pure black, one orange, rounded numerals, a dial.

Built for outdoors and night: OLED black saves battery, and the single international-orange accent reads in sunlight. The distance lives inside a dial whose arc fills as you close in. Feels like a piece of kit, not a website.

E. Live Activity

The game leaves the app. It lives on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island.

The biggest UX idea here, not only a look: players keep the phone locked in a pocket and glance at the lock screen. The in-app screens can then be almost empty. A progress track shows how much of the starting distance you have closed. On the web this becomes a persistent notification plus a pinned mini-bar; true Live Activities need a native wrapper later.

F. Liquid Glass

iOS 26 material. Clear, lens-like glass floating over the real map.

Unlike B, the background is the actual map, always there. Controls are clear glass capsules that bend the map beneath them. In game the map stays blurred under a big glass bubble so navigators can’t see where the target is. Fits iPhones today perfectly.

G. Sky

Apple Weather. The sky changes from night to sunrise as you get closer.

The distance is set like a temperature, in a hairline 100-weight numeral. The whole background is a sky: deep night when you are far, dusk in the middle, bright morning when you are almost there. Beautiful and instantly readable as “am I close?” without reading the number. The hourly strip becomes a recent-distance chart.

H. Rings

Apple Fitness. Three neon rings close as the game goes on.

Outer ring: distance closed. Middle ring: time used. Inner ring: checkpoints or the next elimination. It turns a game into something you “close”, and the results screen becomes a satisfying set of completed rings that is very shareable.
